# Manager Coaching Guide

Use this guide to turn the skill wheel into a focused development conversation. This is coaching, not a test and not a performance rating. The employee owns the first score, the evidence behind it, and the work that follows. The manager brings context, asks for observable evidence, and helps turn broad goals into precise practice.

[Open My Growth](/training/growth) before the conversation so the employee can complete or review the current wheel. If either person needs a refresher on the system, [review the method](/training/method) first.

## The outcome

Leave the conversation with:

1. A shared view of the evidence behind each discussed score.
2. No more than 2–4 focus skills.
3. One precise practice plan for each focus skill.
4. A feedback cadence and 30/60/90-day check-in dates.

If those four things are not clear, the conversation is not finished.

## Before the conversation

Ask the employee to complete the wheel first. Managers should not pre-score the employee or arrive with a hidden answer key. Review the role's real work, recent project situations, and any examples that can make the discussion concrete.

Bring evidence, not labels. Useful evidence includes a marked-up drawing, a decision log, a meeting plan, a procurement update, a daily report, a resolved coordination issue, or an example of the employee teaching someone else. “Strong communicator” is a label. “Set the trade meeting agenda, surfaced two constraints, and documented owners before the meeting closed” is evidence.

Record each example in My Growth as:

- **Situation:** Where and when the work happened.
- **Behavior:** What the employee observably did, without guessing intent.
- **Outcome:** What changed for the work or team.

Choose a private setting and protect 45 minutes. Do not combine this conversation with compensation, discipline, or a surprise performance review.

## The 45-minute coaching conversation

### 1. Set the frame: 5 minutes

Open with the purpose:

> This is your development plan. We are using the wheel to choose a few skills that will make the biggest difference in your work. We will use evidence to calibrate the scores, then agree on precise reps you can own.

Ask:

- Which part of your wheel feels most accurate?
- Which score was hardest to choose?
- Where would stronger capability remove the most friction from your week?

### 2. Calibrate with evidence: 10 minutes

Start with the employee's score. Ask what they can do consistently, what still requires help, and what evidence supports the number. Use the rubric as a shared language:

- **0–20:** Aware of the skill and beginning to recognize the work.
- **30–50:** Can complete parts of the work with an SOP, an AI assistant, or a manager's double-check.
- **60–70:** Can complete most normal situations independently and asks for help on edge cases.
- **80:** Can work independently, document decisions, and escalate with a clear recommendation.
- **100:** Can teach the Alleato way, coach another person, and improve the repeatable process.

Scores are not grades. They are a snapshot of current independence and judgment. A lower, accurate score is more useful than a higher score that cannot be supported.

### 3. Select 2–4 focus skills: 10 minutes

Do not turn every short wedge into a goal. Compare each possible focus area using two questions:

1. How important is this skill to the employee's current role and next level?
2. How large is the gap between current capability and the capability the work requires?

Choose the few skills with the highest combination of importance and gap. If the list is longer than four, ask which improvement would remove a constraint, reduce a recurring error, or create the most leverage for the team.

Also create a stop-doing decision. Growth needs capacity. Name one low-value habit, meeting, or repeated rescue pattern that can be reduced so the employee has room to practice.

### 4. Turn each skill into a precise rep: 15 minutes

“Get better at scheduling” is not a development plan. For each focus skill, define:

- **Action:** The exact work the employee will perform.
- **Frequency:** How often the rep happens.
- **Evidence:** What proves the rep was completed and whether it worked.
- **Resource:** The guide, SOP, example, mentor, or project situation that supports the rep.
- **Feedback:** Who will review it and how quickly.

My Growth records a distinct action and measure for each review:

- **30 days:** Prove the planned reps are happening and use feedback to adjust the next attempt.
- **60 days:** Demonstrate more independent handling of normal situations.
- **90 days:** Show the resulting capability, re-score from evidence, and decide whether the skill remains a focus.

Example:

> For the next four weekly look-ahead meetings, draft the constraint log before the meeting, identify an owner and due date for every open constraint, and send the updated log within two hours. The superintendent reviews the first two drafts the same day. At the 30-day check-in, compare planned work completed and recurring constraint misses.

One real rep repeated with feedback is more valuable than a long list of courses.

### 5. Confirm ownership and cadence: 5 minutes

Have the employee state the plan back in their own words. Confirm:

- The first rep and its due date.
- The evidence that will be brought to the next conversation.
- Where quick feedback will come from.
- The 30-day, 60-day, and 90-day review dates.

The manager provides access, context, and timely feedback. The employee owns the reps, evidence, and follow-through.

## Feedback that moves the wheel

Use three parts:

1. **Observation:** State what happened without guessing intent.
2. **Impact:** Explain what the behavior changed for the work or team.
3. **Next rep:** Name the smallest specific action to repeat or change.

Example:

> The RFI cited the sheet and described the conflict, but it did not include a proposed resolution. That added another review cycle. On the next two RFIs, include one recommended resolution and note the schedule impact before routing them.

Give feedback close to the work. Waiting until a monthly check-in turns a useful correction into a history lesson.

## When you disagree with a score

Do not force an unexplained number. First ask the employee to show the evidence behind the score. Then explain the observable capability you believe is missing or already present.

If you still disagree:

1. Record the two interpretations.
2. Agree on the behavior that would demonstrate the next level.
3. Set a short work-based experiment.
4. Revisit the score with the new evidence.

The goal is shared calibration, not manager victory.

## Common coaching traps

### The employee scores too high

Ask for two recent examples completed at that level of independence. Compare the examples with the rubric. If the evidence is incomplete, agree on the work that would prove the level instead of arguing about confidence.

### The employee scores too low

Ask what they have completed without rescue. Name specific evidence they may be discounting. Then select a rep that stretches the skill without taking ownership away from them.

### The plan has too many priorities

Return to importance and gap. Keep only the 2–4 skills that will materially change current performance or readiness for the next role.

### The practice task is vague

Rewrite it until another manager could observe whether it happened. Add frequency, evidence, and feedback timing.

### The manager keeps rescuing the work

Use the 1-3-1 rule for escalation: bring one problem, three possible solutions, and one recommendation. The manager can challenge the thinking without taking the task back.

## 30/60/90-day check-ins

At each check-in, review evidence before discussing the score.

- **30 days:** Were the reps completed? What feedback changed the next attempt?
- **60 days:** Is the employee handling more normal situations independently? Which edge cases still need support?
- **90 days:** What does the evidence say now? Re-score the focus skills, keep what still matters, and replace any completed focus area with the next highest-leverage skill.

Do not move a score because time passed. Move it when capability and evidence changed.

## Definition of done

The coaching cycle is working when the employee can name the focus skills, show the repeated work, explain what changed from feedback, and make a more independent decision in the next similar situation.
