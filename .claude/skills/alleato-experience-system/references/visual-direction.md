# Alleato Visual Direction

## Architectural-industrial default

The product should feel like a precise working environment shaped by construction artifacts, not a generic SaaS dashboard or science-fiction control room.

### Palette

- white (`#ffffff`) is the default application canvas and primary panel surface;
- graphite and charcoal for structure and primary text;
- neutral grays without warm, beige, cream, parchment, or sepia undertones for secondary surfaces, selection states, and dividers;
- safety orange as the single product accent;
- amber for warning, green for verified completion, and red for critical/destructive state.

State colors are semantic. Do not mix several accents for decoration.

Do not use warm off-white, beige, cream, parchment, ivory, or sepia-tinted backgrounds for application pages, panels, rows, navigation, or selection states. Architectural-industrial direction comes from structure, typography, spacing, construction artifacts, and precise details—not a tinted canvas. When no semantic surface treatment is required, use white.

### Composition

- Use open, asymmetrical layouts when they improve scanning or preserve a working artifact.
- Use compact toolbars, split detail views, timelines, tables, and inspectors around the actual job.
- Let plans, schedules, documents, cost structures, and relationship evidence carry visual interest.
- Prefer type scale, whitespace, alignment, indentation, dividers, and tonal elevation over borders and wrapper cards.
- Keep chrome quiet so project evidence remains dominant.

### Typography and density

- Use the product's established type system; do not introduce display fonts per page.
- Favor crisp labels, readable tabular data, clear numeric alignment, and a restrained hierarchy.
- Small uppercase labels are acceptable only for short metadata categories and must remain readable.
- Dense desktop workbenches may be compact; mobile and coarse-pointer layouts must recompose.

### Motion and depth

- Motion explains arrival, reordering, expansion, causality, or completion.
- Prefer short, interruptible transitions and honor reduced motion.
- Use shadow or elevation only for true layering such as a menu, dialog, drawer, or dragged object.
- Avoid ambient animation, parallax, cursor effects, glowing controls, and animated backgrounds in production workflows.

## How to use the supplied visual references

The dark relationship maps, pipelines, timelines, and node canvases are useful references for:

- spatial relationships;
- dependency inspection;
- evidence lineage;
- pipeline state;
- selection and inspector behavior;
- dense temporal visualization.

They are not references for:

- the global app shell;
- ordinary list, detail, form, or document pages;
- decorative neon, glass, glow, giant illustrations, or card grids;
- top-of-page KPI collections;
- motion without a workflow purpose.

The construction-management brief contributes the stronger default direction: architectural, tactile, technical, and artifact-led. When the two reference families conflict, use the construction direction for the shell and routine workflows; reserve dark/luminous treatment for a bounded analytical canvas whose job requires it.

## Charts and technical instrumentation

- Start with the decision the chart supports.
- Use direct labels, meaningful units, source/freshness, and a text/table fallback where needed.
- Highlight exceptions and deltas, not every series.
- Avoid gradients, pseudo-3D, decorative gauges, unlabeled axes, and color-only legends.
- Do not chart a value that is clearer as a sentence or table cell.
