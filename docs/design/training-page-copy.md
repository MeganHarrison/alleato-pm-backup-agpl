## How It Works

### The Growth Cycle

Growth starts with the desire to improve and the willingness to take responsibility for the work. The Skill Wheel helps you understand where you are today, decide what matters next, and turn improvement into consistent action.

#### Step 1: Take Ownership

Choose to lead your development. Your manager can provide feedback and support, but you own the assessment, the actions, and the follow-through.

#### Step 2: Choose Eight Role-Critical Skills

Identify the eight skills that matter most in your current role. These become the foundation of your Skill Wheel.

#### Step 3: Assess Yourself Honestly

Use the proficiency scale and examples from your actual work to rate your current level in each skill. The goal is an accurate starting point, not a perfect score.

#### Step 4: Choose Your Focus

Review the wheel and select two or three skills that would make the greatest difference in your work. A low score may reveal an opportunity, but importance, current responsibilities, and potential impact should guide the decision.

#### Step 5: Define Precise Actions

For each focus skill, decide exactly what you will practice, how often you will practice it, and what evidence will demonstrate improvement.

#### Step 6: Monitor and Adjust

Track your actions and results consistently. Review progress with your manager and reassess the wheel at 30, 60, and 90 days. Keep what works and adjust what does not.

## Skill Wheel Story

### Build an Honest Starting Point

Choose the eight skills that matter most for your role. Assess your current level in each skill using the proficiency scale and evidence from your actual work.

The wheel gives you a clear picture of where you are today. Shorter wedges are not failures. They are possible opportunities for focused improvement.

### Choose the First Focus Areas

Use the wheel to select two or three high-impact skills to improve first.

The shortest wedges can reveal possible growth areas, but they do not make the decision for you. Choose your focus based on the needs of your role, your current responsibilities, and the difference each skill could make in your work.

### Turn Focus into Consistent Action

Courses build understanding. Consistent practice builds capability.

For each focus skill, define a precise action:

- What will you practice?
- How often will you practice it?
- Where will you apply it in your work?
- What evidence will show improvement?
- Who will provide feedback?

Small actions performed consistently create measurable progress.

### Review the Evidence

Track completed actions, examples from your work, outcomes, and feedback. Review the evidence regularly instead of relying on memory or general impressions.

Reassess your selected skills at 30, 60, and 90 days. Continue reinforcing earlier gains while adding new focus areas when you are ready.

### Demonstrate Readiness

Readiness is demonstrated through consistent, independent performance.

You can handle the work at the expected level, explain the decisions behind it, recognize when to escalate, and bring problems forward before they grow.

The Skill Wheel is a development tool. It helps you and your manager have a clearer coaching conversation, but it is not an automatic performance rating or promotion decision.

## Shared Proficiency Scale

### Aware

I understand what good performance looks like.

### Assisted

I can complete the work with a reliable guide or support.

### Capable

I can own the common path and recognize when I need help.

### Solo

I can handle complexity independently, document my decisions, and escalate appropriately.

### Teach

I consistently perform the work well and help others improve.

## Terminology Rules

- Use **eight role-critical skills** instead of **top eight skills**.
- Use **two or three focus skills** consistently throughout the experience.
- Use **build your Skill Wheel** instead of **take the assessment**.
- Describe lower scores as **growth opportunities**, not weaknesses or failures.
- Treat the wheel as a development and coaching tool, not an automatic performance or promotion decision.
- Connect every focus skill to a precise action, a cadence, evidence, and feedback.
